# Why a coupon did or did not reach a customer

How to answer «لماذا لم يصل أي كود؟» from the platform's own records, in the
order the gates actually run. Written after a Tenant 1 trial where the
assistant reported no available codes while the merchant's records held
eight shareable ones, and nothing in the deployed build could say which gate
had taken them.

## The gates, in order

A coupon reaches `list_shareable_promotions` only by passing all of these.
Each one that closes is now counted under its own reason in the tool's
`withheld` map and in the `[PROMOTION_PROJECTION]` log line.

| # | Gate | Where | Reason recorded |
|---|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | Merchant's AI coupon policy is enabled | `coupons_dashboard.ai_policy.enabled` | result `status=denied` |
| 2 | The record is usable — an id, a kind, and a code for a coupon | promotion truth row | `unusable_record` |
| 3 | A personal code belongs to *this* customer | `bound_customer_id` | `personal_to_another_customer` |
| 4 | Enough life left for the merchant's minimum | `ai_policy.min_remaining_hours` | `expiring_before_store_minimum` |
| 5 | The rung is one the merchant lets the assistant read | `ai_policy.allowed_levels` | `level_not_allowed_by_store_policy` |
| 6 | The customer stands on that rung | `services.coupon_entitlement_read` | `level_not_earned_by_customer` |
| 7 | No rung: the merchant published it to an AI surface | `source_type` + `allocation_channel` | `not_published_for_general_use` |

Gate 7 is the one that surprises people. A coupon carrying no
`coupon_level` is not thereby a public offer — the record is simply
declining to name a rung. It reaches a customer only when the merchant both
created it in Nahla (`source_type="manual"`) and placed it on a surface the
assistant reads (`allocation_channel` in `ai`, `shared`). A coupon synced in
from the store platform (`imported`), or generated by an automation
(`system`), expresses no such intent.

A `NULL` `source_type` column reads as `manual` (`_row_to_coupon_fact`), so an
old row is not withheld for want of a source. A `NULL` `allocation_channel`
reads as the empty string, which is in neither channel, so the channel is the
one of the two that an old row will fail.

**Known gap.** The dashboard's own «كوبون ترويجي عام» sends neither
`coupon_level` nor `allocation_channel` (`Coupons.tsx`, `CreateStoreCouponModal`:
both fields are sent only when the purpose is «متاح لتخصيص الذكاء الاصطناعي»).
So the most ordinary thing a merchant can do — create a general promotional
coupon — produces a row gate 7 withholds. The merchant *has* made an explicit
declaration; the platform does not record it anywhere the gate can read. See
"What is still missing" below.

## Reading the customer's standing

`resolve_level_entitlement` answers one question — which rung has this
customer reached — and says how firmly. Seven outcomes, and only two of them
are determinations:

| `reason` | Determined | What happened |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| `entitled_by_order_count` | yes | The ladder was applied to a real count |
| `no_entitled_level` | yes | Counted, and the count reaches no enabled rung |
| `identity_not_established` | no | No customer was identified in this conversation |
| `customer_record_unavailable` | no | The id is present; no customer of this tenant carries it |
| `order_history_not_searchable` | no | The record exists and carries no phone the order index can be searched by |
| `order_history_unreadable` | no | The read raised |
| `level_policy_unreadable` | no | The merchant's ladder could not be read |

`order_history_not_searchable` is the one that used to hide. Orders are
indexed by normalized phone (`CustomerIntelligenceService.order_lookup_key`);
a customer record carrying none produces a count of zero from a search that
never ran. Read as "no purchases", that zero hands a first-purchase welcome
to someone with years of orders and tells them they are new. It is now a
failure to determine, and the issuance half refuses it under the same name.

**Which customer.** The commerce runtime takes the customer from the
conversation row (`Conversation.customer_id`, passed through
`run_commerce_runtime_turn`). The trusted-context shadow resolves its own,
from the phone via `build_order_context(...).identity.customer_id`. They are
different resolutions and can disagree, so a shadow line saying a customer was
found is not evidence that the runtime had one. `identity_not_established`
means the *conversation* carried no customer, whatever the shadow saw.

`raw_orders` and `excluded_orders` travel with every determination, so
"six orders, none of them countable" is distinguishable from "no orders".
Excluded means the countability policy set the order aside — cancelled,
refunded, failed, voided, abandoned, draft (`order_countability_policy`).

Orders are counted **per tenant**: `Order.tenant_id == tenant_id`. Purchases
made from a different merchant on this platform are not this merchant's
history and never count toward its ladder. Orders whose `source` marks them
internal end-to-end traffic are excluded from the index entirely.

## Reading the shadow's counters

`[TRUSTED_CONTEXT_SHADOW]` reports `coupon_count`, `eligible_coupon_count` and
`unavailable_coupon_reason_codes` from `coupon_offer_loader`. Two cautions:

* `coupon_count` is capped — the loader reads `db.query(Coupon)…limit(50)`, so
  `50` means "at least fifty", never "the store has fifty".
* Its `eligible` is a different question from this document's. It asks whether
  a code could be *allocated* to this customer (active, unexpired, unapplied,
  within usage limits, product restrictions satisfied). It says nothing about
  whether the assistant may *mention* a code, which is what the gates above
  decide.

## What to read in production

```
[PROMOTION_TRUTH]       tenant=… candidate_count=… shareable=… offers=…
[PROMOTION_PROJECTION]  tenant=… considered=… kept=… withheld=<reason:count,…> standing=… level=… determined=…
[COUPON_ENTITLEMENT]    tenant=… outcome=… …        # only on a failure to determine
```

`[PROMOTION_TRUTH]` says what the merchant's records held.
`[PROMOTION_PROJECTION]` says how many survived and which gate took the rest.
Between them an empty answer is fully explained without a transcript. None of
the three carries a code, a customer identifier, or a phone number.

The same counts reach the model in the tool observation, under `entitlement`
and `withheld`, on the empty and failed paths as well as the successful one —
so the assistant can say *why* it has nothing rather than only that it has
nothing.

## What is still missing

1. **A merchant act that gate 7 can read.** The dashboard lets a merchant
   declare a coupon «كوبون ترويجي عام» and then stores that declaration only
   as `ai_allocatable: false` with no channel. Recording it — so the platform
   can tell "the merchant published this for everyone" from "the record
   happens to name no rung" — is what closes the gap without loosening the
   gate. The alternative, treating an absent level as public, is exactly what
   gate 7 exists to prevent.

2. **A welcome coupon the merchant can actually shape.** The
   `first_purchase` rule is a real, persisted, entitlement-gated dashboard
   setting with its own `discount_type`, `discount_value`, `validity_days`,
   `min_order_amount` and `max_uses`. None of those five is read anywhere.
   `resolve_coupon_level_for_order_count` authorizes the **bronze level
   entry** and returns bronze's economics, so a merchant who configures a 15%
   one-day welcome gets whatever bronze says. The merchant can switch the
   welcome on and off and cannot say what it is.

3. **A way to mark a coupon as the welcome.** There is no such marking. A
   first-purchase customer resolves to bronze and sees the bronze rung's
   codes, indistinguishable from an ordinary bronze reward.

4. **Issuance.** `CUSTOMER_COUPON_LIVE_ISSUANCE` is `False`; nothing is
   generated for a first purchase today. A merchant relying on the welcome
   must have a bronze coupon already in the pool.
